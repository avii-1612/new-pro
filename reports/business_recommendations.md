# Business Recommendations

These follow directly from the findings in `business_insights.md`.

**1. Keep discounts at 10% or under where possible.** Margin drops from 35.4% at no discount to just 12.3% once you're at 21-30%. Deep discounts should be reserved for stock that's genuinely not moving, not used as a default promo.

**2. Protect the Electronics margin instead of just chasing its volume.** It's the biggest revenue driver but the weakest on margin (12%). Bundling accessories or extended warranties alongside phones/laptops could help lift profitability without sacrificing sales.

**3. Put more marketing budget behind Beauty and Fashion.** At 45% and 39% margin respectively, extra spend here likely converts to profit more efficiently than pushing Electronics harder.

**4. Build some kind of loyalty program around the High Value RFM segment.** These customers already account for a big chunk of revenue. Even small perks (early access, free shipping thresholds) should have a good return given how concentrated the value already is.

**5. Stock up ahead of the Q4 peak (Oct-Dec).** Revenue climbs reliably through this window every year. Running out of top Electronics SKUs during this stretch would mean missing sales at the highest-demand time of year.

**6. Try a targeted, smaller promo during the May-June slump instead of a blanket discount.** The seasonal dip is predictable, so a modest, well-aimed offer might smooth things out without repeating the margin damage that comes with heavier discounting.

**7. Take a look at the lowest-revenue Beauty and Books SKUs.** Some of them are still profitable per unit, so this isn't about cutting them outright, more about deciding whether they deserve continued shelf space and catalog focus versus higher performers.
